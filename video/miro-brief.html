The context in one minute

The course: Corporate Entrepreneurship (IE EMBA). We act as L'Oréal's innovation team presenting to the CEO. Milestone 4 is an open innovation pilot with an existing startup or partner.

Our proposal: PRO•GO, a 12-week hair-care subscription. The client's own colourist from her partner salon visits her at home twice (week 2 and week 10), with a hair scan before and after. The external partner is ServiceMarket, a Dubai home-services operator, which runs booking, payment, insurance and the salon's permit paperwork.

B

Capability gap and build / buy / partner / invest

Answers: forum Q1 · professor "is external collaboration justified?" · peers "do they need a partner at all?"

Paste

The capability we lack: getting a licensed, insured, permitted professional into a private home in Dubai, on time. L'Oréal has the science, the products and the salons that trust us. It has never run home visits. Build: years to set up operations, permits and insurance. Too slow for a 2027 test. Buy: owning a home-service company would put L'Oréal between the salon and its client, the opposite of what salons need. Invest (minority stake): no strategic reason before the pilot proves demand. Partner: an operator that already does this every day, and works through salons. → ServiceMarket.

D

Collaboration model: venture client

Answers: forum Q3 · professor "name the vehicle" · CEO "is the partner credible?"

Paste

Model: venture-client pilot. L'Oréal buys ServiceMarket's service (paid per visit, 90 days). No equity, no joint venture, no acquisition before the pilot proves demand. We ask: to be the only colour subscription on its platform in our two pilot communities during the test. What ServiceMarket gets: the L'Oréal name behind its service; a door to salons it could never sign one by one; paid visits from day one. What the salon gets: keeps the client, earns on visits it could not make alone (target +10 % income per partner salon by 2030). What L'Oréal gets: in-use data on real hair, in real homes, over 12 weeks, and a new recurring service. Validation before launch: one call with ServiceMarket's partnerships team to test appetite for exclusivity and per-visit pricing.

fact +10 % salon income by 2030: M2 dashboard. assumption per-visit payment and exclusivity are Team D proposals, not agreed.

H

Governance, review rhythm, scaling

Answers: forum Q4 · CEO "who is the one leader?" · fixes an inconsistency

Paste

Owner: one PRO•GO lead in the PRO•GO venture unit, inside Professional Products Middle East. Holds the salon relationships and the pilot budget. Rhythm: monthly reviews during the 90-day pilot; the Innovation Board reviews quarterly after the pilot. Go: ≥ 40 % book a second visit within 90 days and every visit makes money → next €7m at the Q4 2027 review; add salons. Change: people come back but the money doesn't work → new price, new services or a new deal with ServiceMarket. Fall back: salons won't send colourists out → test a salon subscription instead. Stop: two missed monthly reviews in a row. At scale: the venture unit hands over to Professional Products Middle East with its own P&L.

Why this matters: the video says "two missed reviews in a row" and "reviews every quarter". Two quarters is longer than a 90-day pilot, so the board makes the reviews monthly during the pilot.

I

Partner-salon offer

Answers: salon owners · classmates "channel conflict" · our own fall-back rule
  • Payout per visit at least what the colourist earns in the salon for the same time (travel included)
  • Off-peak slots first (weekday mornings)
  • Permits, insurance and transport handled through ServiceMarket
  • In the contract: the client stays the salon's; the salon's name on every booking; the salon sees the scan results (with consent)
  • Non-solicitation, so colourists don't take PRO•GO clients private
  • Weekly payment
  • Starter kit, training and "PRO•GO certified", and a minimum number of bookings during the 90 days

Team D proposal all terms.

J

Colourist charter

Answers: service quality, repeat visits, safety
  • Paid per 3.5-hour visit block including travel, at a premium over a salon appointment, plus tips; 2–3 home visits a day at most
  • Kit supplied: portable rinse unit, daylight lamp, cape, floor mats, chemical disposal bag
  • Scan, treatment and advice happen while the colour sets
  • Safety: verified addresses, check-in and check-out in the app, a contact line, the right to leave; women clients first
  • Permit and approvals on her phone; patch test 48 hours before (already in the prototype)
  • The hair score is Layla's journey, never the colourist's performance review
  • Two taps in the app per visit; scan under one minute; named in the booking; "PRO•GO certified"
K

Customer promise (Layla)

Answers: why she says yes, and what would stop her

Paste

Your own colourist, at home, in private. A named backup from the same salon if needed. Setup and clean-up included: cape, floor mat, rinse. Your score in plain words: colour that lasts, less breakage, shine. Your data: you see it, you can delete it anytime. Pause or cancel in one message. A WhatsApp check-in at week 4.

Why it matters: for many clients in Dubai, privacy and a female professional at home are a stronger reason to buy than convenience alone.

O

FAQ for peer feedback

Short answers, each pointing to its panel; reuse them in forum replies
  • Why not Urban Company? Its brand stands at the door, so the client moves to the app. We need the salon in front. (C)
  • Does each visit make money? That is our go rule; the table shows the costs per visit. (G)
  • Who owns the client? The salon, by contract. L'Oréal sees the hair data with consent. (I, K)
  • 90 days but "two missed reviews"? Reviews are monthly during the pilot. (H)
  • Why Dubai first? Home services are already common, salons are dense, and the M1–M3 work chose the Middle East market.
  • Is the score valid? Not a medical measurement; Layla's numbers are illustrative; the pilot measures real change. (F, L)
